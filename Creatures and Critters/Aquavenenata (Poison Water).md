Climate: Desert

Domain: Multicellula
Kingdom: Spiriare
Phylum: Pelisrigidum
Class: Sexcrura
Order: Unamutatio
Family: Aquavenenata

Diet: Juveniles will eat roots, carapaces, plants, and anything else they can reach. Adults with primarily eat sap and cactus juices, along with carapaces when available.

Description: Aquavenenata Juveniles will seek out water sources and bloat themselves, mixing a mild poison that they themselves are immune to to dissuade predators. This often doesn't work as the poison isn't lethal except in extreme doses and more often then not only leads to a mild stomach ache. They have not needed to evolve this out however as they breed in such massive quantities that significant amounts survive each season. Adults gain the benefits of flight along with long proboscises that they use to pierce cacti flesh so they can feed upon the water inside, no longer needing to store as much inside of themselves. Both stages of Aquavenenata are vital food sources for desert ecosystems.

Aquavenenata adults are able to asexually reproduce by depositing thousands of eggs into the cacti that they drink from, this leads to extremely high populations which are only balanced out due to their lack of defensive measures as juveniles, and lack of nutritional value in the adults, leading to extremely high predation rates. The eggs hatch after a few days of pupation and then spend 1 - 2 weeks scavenging for nutrients before beginning their metamorphosis phase, where they spend a week buried underground transforming into their adult forms. A single adult can lay hundreds of thousands of eggs in its lifetime, only a tenth of which become adults and continue the cycle.
